The Identity Lab is hiring multiple undergraduates to work with us on queer and trans projects over the summer. Help carry out important research, while you learn how to do research on people and the internet, including interviewing, design methods, and more.

Summer research assistants can expect to be a major part of the research and will learn practical research skills on the job. The summer will start with a methods bootcamp, and then RAs will jump directly into live data collection with the project’s principle investigator. These are UROP-funded positions for the full summer and can be done completely remotely. These positions are open to all CU Boulder students in any major, but may be most relevant for those studying Information Science, Computer Science, Communication, and Gender/Sexuality Studies. Prior research experience is not required. Personal or professional experience with LGBTQ+, and especially trans, communities, eating disorders, online communities, and online community management/moderation is preferred, but not required.

Summer research assistants will have the opportunity to work on two projects with the lab’s postdoctoral associates, Dr. Michael Ann DeVito and Dr. Jessica Feuston:

**Making Online Dating Safe and Effective for Queer Cis and Trans Women**  
_Principle Investigator: Dr. Michael Ann DeVito_

Do dating apps bother you? Does it seem to you like queer women, in particular, run into serious problems with these apps? What if we started from scratch, and used design to help queer women themselves say what a dating app should be?

We are looking for talented undergraduates who want to learn how to do design work via talking to our communities directly, and pick up some skill at online moderation, value-based design, and qualitative, human-centered approaches to technology in the process. Join us as we engage in a community setting, and explicitly center the needs of transgender women and nonbinary people as a starting point, not an afterthought. Along the way, gain mentorship on research and research careers from established, openly-queer scientists, including the PI, a queer, transgender woman. Finally: expect us to learn from you, because your identity is part of good research, too.

**Understanding the Online Experiences of Trans People with Eating Disorders**  
_Principle Investigator: Dr. Jessica Feuston_

Are you interested in designing better online experiences for trans people with eating disorders?

We are looking for interested undergraduates who want to learn how to use qualitative research methods to center the experiences, perspectives, needs, and concerns of trans people with eating disorders who participate online. You will have opportunities to engage with communities and work on qualitative research with an established research team, where your perspective and identity are a valued part of the process. You will work with mentors who are dedicated to your success on this project and to your career as a researcher. We also prioritize mental health and wellbeing as part of our research practice. Expect to learn how conduct research on difficult subjects, including mental illness, safely and with support.

**If you are interested in working with us on either or both of these projects, email [michaelann@colorado.edu](michaelann@colorado.edu) and [jessica.feuston@colorado.edu](jessica.feuston@colorado.edu) with the following:**
1.	Which project you are more interested in of the two above
2.	Your class year and major
3.	A brief (less than 200 words) statement of why you are interested in learning how to do this kind of research.
4.	A brief (less than 300 words) description of any relevant prior personal or professional experience (including experience with LGBTQ+ communities, eating disorders, and online community management/moderation)
5.	A resume or CV
