As AI systems improve in both breadth and depth of performance, they make it possible to create increasingly realistic agents—including agents modeled on specific people. We anticipate that within our lifetimes it may become common practice for people to create "generative ghosts": AI agents that represent the deceased and that the living can converse with.

These technologies are likely to reshape existing practices—and give rise to novel ones—around end-of-life planning, remembrance, and legacy, with profound legal, economic, emotional, and religious ramifications. This project examines how people understand and engage with AI afterlives, the design considerations they raise, and the benefits and risks of building agents that speak for those who have died.

Since naming that design space, we have run the first user-experience study of people in conversation with these systems, and a follow-up study is in data collection now. Using AI to help people explore personal, family, and cultural archives is a follow-up area we've been considering as well.
